When the river falls, freight costs don’t wait.

The data to see it coming is public, but it is scattered across agencies, formats and time scales. Nobody puts water levels and barge rates in one view.

Lighter loads

Shallow channels force barges to carry less cargo and tows to run with fewer barges.

Delays and groundings

Queues build at locks, channels get restricted, and vessels wait for dredging.

Rates jump in weeks

Less capacity meets harvest demand, and spot rates climb faster than shippers can react.

One gauge, one rate, one clear warning.

1. Collect

Daily river stage from USGS and weekly barge rates from the USDA Grain Transportation Report.

2. Connect

Water levels are averaged by week and joined to the rate series, so both share one timeline.

3. Alert

If the level stays below the threshold for 7 days, RiverRate flags rising-rate risk and shows what happened after the same signal in past years.
